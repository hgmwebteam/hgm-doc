How a client-approved site on its netlify.app address becomes the client's live site on their own domain — audited, keyed, paid-path-tested and cut over. 01Purpose and scope
Purpose
Takes a site the client has approved on its *.netlify.app address and makes it ready to be a business: search and answer-engine ready, accessible, fast on every page, correct at every screen size, legally complete, wired to the client's live payment, email, booking and CRM accounts, proven with a test booking, and moved onto the client's real domain without taking their email down.
In scope
SEO and GEO. Accessibility to WCAG 2.2 AA. The Lighthouse loop. BrowserStack and real-phone checks. Legal and policy pages. Filling any keys still missing. The HGM and Meta pixels. The money path. A test booking. The DNS cutover. Confirming the site is live.
Out of scope
The build, the reviews and the client's approval — WEB-004. Google Analytics 4, Search Console and Hotjar — WEB-003, from the day the site is live.

This SOP starts when the account manager tells you the client has approved the site — WEB-004 task 8.6. It is not started from Asana. It ends where WEB-003 phase 1 begins: the site answers on the client's domain and the repository is proven to be the live site.

This is a field-test draft

Every gap still open is marked [NEEDS INPUT] in place. Run the first launch against it, write down what actually happened at each flag, and hand the answers to the Web & Content Specialist. Do not treat a flagged task as an instruction.

Always true

Five standing rules

True on every launch.

1
Money work happens in Claude Fable, on test keys
Prices, coupons, checkout, webhooks and keys: Fable, Stripe test mode, never a real charge.
2
The client's email survives the cutover
Write down every MX and TXT record before DNS moves; prove mail arrives after.
3
Nothing is verified until it is seen on the live domain
Every check on netlify.app is repeated on the real domain in phase 10.
4
Claude's audit is checked, not trusted
Every audit prompt is followed by a check you do yourself. Yours is the one that counts.
5
Never invent a value
Keys and IDs come from hgmportal.com, 1Password or the client — never from another client's file.

The MX and TXT records are the client's email

MX routes the client's mail; TXT carries SPF, DKIM and DMARC, which keep it out of spam. Whatever method task 9.8 uses, those records must exist unchanged afterwards. Moving the nameservers to Netlify DNS removes them unless they are recreated first.
